## Source

Synthea sample data, "100 Sample Synthetic Patient Records, FHIR R4" on https://synthea.mitre.org/downloads (the page says it is moving to https://synthetichealth.github.io/downloads.html): `https://synthetichealth.github.io/synthea-sample-data/downloads/latest/synthea_sample_data_fhir_latest.zip`, downloaded 2026-10-13 (29,595,199 bytes, sha256 `56cb9e49f7ba6ad4e61c40aa80999f8c10a710823fed1becdf2502053777a521`; 111 files dated 2026-08-18: 109 patient bundles plus `hospitalInformation1787069559744.json` and `practitionerInformation1787069559744.json`). The seven files above are the seven smallest patient bundles in that archive, chosen by size alone. The `latest` link is a moving target, so the archive's size, hash and date pin what was used.

## Terms

The patients do not exist: Synthea generates its population from statistics and disease modules, and the digits in the names are its marker of synthetic data. The Synthea downloads page states: "The data is free from cost, privacy, and security restrictions. It can be used without restriction for a variety of secondary uses in academia, research, industry, and government." It asks that Synthea be cited as: Jason Walonoski, Mark Kramer, Joseph Nichols, Andre Quina, Chris Moesel, Dylan Hall, Carlton Duffett, Kudakwashe Dube, Thomas Gallagher, Scott McLachlan, *Synthea: An approach, method, and software mechanism for generating synthetic patients and the synthetic electronic health care record*, Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association, Volume 25, Issue 3, March 2018, Pages 230–238, https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocx079.
